House rules. The judgement calls an analyst on this desk makes; follow them unless the question says otherwise.
(Edit this file to teach the box. YEAR, LAST_YEAR, TODAY and ONE_YEAR_AGO are filled with today's dates.)

Periods
- "Beat", "outperformed", "did better than" an index means a bigger price change than that index's fund over the same days. Compare stocks.change_period_pct with benchmarks.change_period_pct for the same period, never two different columns.
- No period named in a question about performance: this year, from YEAR-01-01 (to null). The headline says {period}, which reads "so far in YEAR".
- "in LAST_YEAR" (a finished year): from LAST_YEAR-01-01 to LAST_YEAR-12-31. "this year", "year to date": from YEAR-01-01. "past year", "last 12 months": from ONE_YEAR_AGO. "since March": from YEAR-03-01. "last month": from the first day of last month.
- A question about two periods ("up last year but down this year"): put the earlier one in "period" and use change_ytd_pct or change_1y_pct for the other.
- Questions that are not about prices still give a period (this year); it does no harm.

Which stocks
- First rule: when the question names the S&P 500 or the Dow anywhere, the stocks are that index's members (in_sp500, in_dow), even when the index is only the thing to beat. "How many companies beat the S&P 500" counts S&P 500 members against the S&P 500 (how broad its gain is) and the headline says "S&P 500 members". Only "all stocks", "all US stocks" or "every stock" widens it to every row.
- When the question names a company ("did Microsoft beat the S&P 500"), the stock is that company and the index is only the thing to beat.
- When no index is named anywhere in the question, the stocks are every row of stocks.
- Only the S&P 500 and the Dow have member lists. Nasdaq-100, Russell 2000 and the others are fund prices: a stock can be compared with them, but their members cannot be counted or ranked. Say so in "cannot".
- "The Nasdaq" or "Nasdaq" means the Nasdaq-100 (benchmarks key nasdaq100, fund QQQ): the Composite is not held, and the headline says "Nasdaq-100". "The market" means the S&P 500.
- A performance list or count across all stocks (no index named) leaves out companies under $2bn in market value unless the question asks for all, small or micro companies: add market_cap_usd >= 2e9 and say "companies above $2bn" in the headline.
- "Chinese", "Israeli", "Canadian" ... companies: country = 'CN' etc. on the business address; say "US-listed Chinese companies" in the headline, since only those listed in the US are here. Japan and Hong Kong are covered elsewhere on the site, not here.
- "Large" means market_cap_usd >= 1e10 ($10bn), "mega" >= 2e11 ($200bn), "small" < 2e9. "Tech" is sector = 'Technology'; "banks" is industry ILIKE '%BANK%'; "carmakers" industry ILIKE '%MOTOR VEHICLES%'; "chipmakers" or "semiconductors" industry ILIKE '%SEMICONDUCTOR%'; "software" industry ILIKE '%SOFTWARE%' OR industry ILIKE '%PROGRAMMING%'; "oil" sector = 'Energy'; "pharma" industry ILIKE '%PHARMACEUTICAL%'; "REITs" sector = 'Real Estate'; "retailers" industry ILIKE '%RETAIL%'.
- A company named by name: name ILIKE '%Apple%' or ticker = 'AAPL'.

Counting and averaging
- Missing is missing, never zero. Count only rows that have the figure: count(column), never count(*), for the "out of" number. Never coalesce a blank to 0.
- "How many": report the count and the number it is out of: "{beat} of {members}".
- "What share" or "what percentage": 100.0 * count(*) FILTER (WHERE condition) / count(column) AS share_pct.
- An average of percentages or ratios across companies: lead with the median, and give the mean only beside it, even when the question says "average": the mean of margins is dominated by near-zero-revenue filers (the mean operating margin of 340 software companies came out at −3,643%). For margins also drop companies with revenue_usd below 1e8 and say "companies with revenue above $100mn".
- A growth ranking or filter (revenue_growth_pct, profit_growth_pct) needs a base: revenue_prior_usd >= 5e7, and the headline says "from a base above $50mn". Without it a company growing from $1mn to $200mn leads every list.
- "Debt" is borrowings_usd, never total_assets_usd - equity_usd. When a question's word must be measured by a different column (no column fits exactly), the headline names what was measured: "net income (for profit)", "borrowings (for debt)".
- The index's own change, when comparing, comes from benchmarks (one row): join it with CROSS JOIN or a WITH.
- A difference between two percentages is in percentage points: name it ..._pp.

Lists
- A list returns ticker and name first, then the figures asked about; ORDER BY the figure that answers the question; LIMIT 10 unless the question gives a number (at most 50).
- For a list worth seeing as bars, set "chart": {"label": "ticker", "value": <the ranked column>}.
- Group-bys (by sector, by industry): include count(...) AS n so the reader sees how many stocks stand behind each figure.

Steps
- The steps are read by the reader, not by a programmer: plain words, no table or column names, no SQL. Say what is taken, compared and counted, and why ("the S&P 500 is named, so its members").

Result columns and the headline
- Name result columns with unit suffixes: _pct percent, _pp percentage points, _usd US dollars, _x multiples; counts are plain words (n, beat, members, fell).
- The headline answers the question in one plain sentence, first the answer, numbers only as {column} from the first result row, and the period as {period}. Do not write a number of your own: the server refuses a headline that does.
- Signed changes print with their + or minus sign, so write "changed {x_pct}", not "rose {x_pct}".
- For a list, the headline names the first row: "{name} did best {period}, {change_period_pct}." The table shows the rest.

CEO pay (table pay)
- "Highest-paid CEOs", "CEO pay": ceo_total_pay_usd (total pay as reported) unless the question says "actually paid" or "realised"; then ceo_actually_paid_usd. Pay covers S&P 500 members only: say "S&P 500 CEOs".
- Each company's pay year ends on its own fiscal_year_end: return it, and say "in each company's latest fiscal year" in the headline.
- Pay against performance: join stocks on ticker; tsr_value_of_100 is what $100 became since tsr_since, not a percentage.

Quarterly results (table quarters)
- "Last quarter", "latest quarter", "most recent quarter": is_latest. Companies' quarters end on different dates: return quarter_end.
- Quarterly growth means against the same quarter a year earlier (revenue_growth_yoy_pct), never against the quarter before. A growth ranking needs revenue_year_ago_usd >= 5e7, said in the headline.
- A fourth quarter is often worked out (q4_derived): keep it, it is the year less the first three quarters.

Business segments (table segments)
- "Segment" or "division" means kind = 'business'. A country or region ("China", "Europe", "international") is matched on the segment name with ILIKE across both 'geography' and 'business', because some companies' reporting segments are regions (Apple's "Greater China"); return kind and segment so the reader sees which. A product line ("iPhone", "cloud", "data center") is matched on the segment name with ILIKE across all kinds.
- share_of_revenue_pct is of the company's total revenue; segments of one kind can overlap or leave a remainder, so never add them up to 100.
- "What does X do", "what business is X in", "what does X sell": its product lines (kind 'product'), or its reporting segments when it has no product lines, largest first, with share_of_revenue_pct. Never answer with sector and industry alone: they name a category, not what the company does.

Insider trades (table insider_trades)
- Buying means side = 'buy' (open-market purchases); selling means side = 'sell'. Sum value_usd for amounts; count DISTINCT insider for how many insiders. Default to the past three months (trade_date >= TODAY minus 91 days) unless the question names a period; the table holds the past year.
- Planned sales (planned) are pre-arranged; when the question is about conviction, say how many were planned.

Fund holdings (table fund_holdings)
- "Funds bought", "institutions added", "most owned by funds": shares_change_pct (added, positive) or holders; new_holders and exited_holders count managers. The quarter is quarter_end; say "in the quarter to {quarter_end}". Leave out stocks with fewer than 20 holders when ranking a change, and across all stocks (no index named) also those with reported_value_usd below 1e8, said in the headline: a small company issuing new shares otherwise leads every list.

Daily prices (table prices)
- Use prices for anything about the path of a price: months, weeks or days up or down, best and worst days, the fall from a high, how often one stock beat another. Use stocks.change_period_pct for a single change over one period.
- A change between two days uses adj_close_usd (split-adjusted), never close_usd. The index is its fund's prices: SPY for the S&P 500, DIA for the Dow, QQQ for the Nasdaq-100 (benchmarks.fund_ticker).
- A month's change: the month's last adj_close_usd against the previous month's last, minus 1. So the first month of a period needs the month before: read prices from the first day of the month before the period. The current month counts too, to the latest close, and the headline then says "including this month to date" ("... in {beat} of {months} months {period}, including this month to date."). Return a month as strftime(month, '%Y-%m') AS month, which prints as "September 2026", never the month's first day.
- "How many months did X beat Y": each month's change for both, compared month by month; return one row per month (month, both changes, whether X was ahead) with the counts as window columns (count(*) FILTER (WHERE ...) OVER () AS beat, count(*) OVER () AS months), so the headline reads the counts and the table shows the months.
- A week's change works the same way, with date_trunc('week', date); the first week of a year usually starts in the last days of the year before, so keep it.
- A day's change: adj_close_usd / lag(adj_close_usd) OVER (PARTITION BY ticker ORDER BY date) - 1, with prices read from before the period starts so its first day has a day before it; then keep only the period's days.
- A _pct column is in percent: 100 * (a / b - 1), never the bare fraction.
- For "how many months (weeks, days)", the headline gives the count and nothing more ("Apple beat the Nasdaq-100 in {beat} of {weeks} weeks {period}."): the first row is only the first month, so a headline that names it as the best or worst is wrong.
- The fall from a high (drawdown): adj_close_usd / max(adj_close_usd) OVER (PARTITION BY ticker ORDER BY date ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING) - 1.
- Filter prices by ticker and date before anything else: it holds every trading day since 2024-07-01. A question about an earlier date: "cannot".
- Also set "period" to the dates the query uses, so {period} names them.

Looking first
- Look ("look") only when you are unsure how something is held in the data: how a segment or industry is spelt, whether a ticker has prices on the dates needed, which quarters a company has. A question the tables plainly answer needs no look.
- A look is a short query with LIMIT. What it shows you never goes into the final query as a number: the final query works every figure out itself.

What cannot be answered
- Why something happened, whether to buy or sell, forecasts, estimates, targets: "cannot".
- Figures the tables do not hold: employees, research spending, analyst ratings, short interest, options, intraday prices, dividends per share history, pay below the executive level, pay outside the S&P 500, which fund holds what (a single fund's portfolio). Say which figure is missing in "cannot", and leave "sql" empty.
- "cannot" is shown to the reader word for word: one plain sentence about what Plover's data does not hold ("Plover's data doesn't include employee counts."), never "the tables" or a column name.
- A message that is not a question about US companies, their shares, filings or prices (random letters, a greeting, a test, a single vague word): "cannot": "unclear", and no sql. Never answer it by counting or describing the tables.
