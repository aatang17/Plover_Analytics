For Researchers

Official statistics and company filings for Japan, the United States and Hong Kong, exactly as published: load them into Stata, R or Python, freeze the release you used, and cite it.

Overview

Every figure on this site is the publisher's own value, read from the agency's file and archived with its checksum. Rates such as year-on-year changes are calculated from those values, and each one states its formula. Every release is kept, so any series can be read as it stood on an earlier date. Reading pages, downloading files and calling the API are free and need no account or key.

Coverage

  • Japan, labour and population: the Monthly Labour Survey (wages, hours, employment) from 1952, the Labour Force Survey from 1953, the job openings-to-applicants ratio from 1963, monthly births, deaths, marriages and divorces from 2005 (preliminary and revised), population by single year of age from 1980, and population by prefecture with age, sex, nationality and migration.
  • Japan, prices and the economy: the CPI at item level from 1970 and all items from 1946, GDP, the Bank of Japan, government finance, trade and visitor arrivals.
  • Japanese companies, in English: cross-shareholdings, 5% shareholder filings, buybacks, boards and pay, shareholder votes and business risks, read from annual reports and filings on EDINET.
  • United States and Hong Kong: SEC financials, CEO pay, insider trades, 13F holdings, Treasury yields and BLS prices and jobs; Hong Kong's official statistics.

The full list, with how far back each dataset runs and when it was last updated, is on Datasets.

Loading Data into Stata, R or Python

Charts that draw published series as they are have a Copy Code button beside Download CSV. It writes one command for the series, measure and dates on screen. The same commands, for Japan's job openings-to-applicants ratio since 1963:

Stata

* Source: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, Employment Referrals for General Workers
import delimited "https://ploveranalytics.com/api/v1/job-openings-jp/observations?series=sa.active.incl_part&start=1963-01&as_of=2026-10-30&format=csv&notes=0", clear
gen month = monthly(period, "YM")
format month %tm
tsset month

R

jo <- read.csv("https://ploveranalytics.com/api/v1/job-openings-jp/observations?series=sa.active.incl_part&start=1963-01&as_of=2026-10-30&format=csv",
               comment.char = "#")

Python

import pandas as pd
jo = pd.read_csv("https://ploveranalytics.com/api/v1/job-openings-jp/observations?series=sa.active.incl_part&start=1963-01&as_of=2026-10-30&format=csv",
                 comment="#")

The file opens with lines starting # that name the source table, the release, the formula and the date it was retrieved; R and Python skip them with the comment option. Stata cannot, so its command adds notes=0, which leaves them out and turns the series codes into Stata variable names (sa.active.incl_part becomes sa_active_incl_part). Blank cells are values not published; they are never zero. Up to eight series fit in one request; the API Reference covers the rest.

Freezing a Release

Government series are revised: seasonally adjusted figures are recalculated every January, and agencies replace their files. Add as_of=YYYY-MM-DD to any data address and it returns the values as they stood on that date, today and in any later year. The date covers the whole day, so the release that arrived that morning is included. Copy Code adds it for you, set to the day the release on screen arrived; untick Freeze to fetch the latest values instead.

Two addresses show the history behind a series:

  • /api/v1/job-openings-jp/releases: every release of a dataset, with the date it arrived and the checksum of the source file.
  • /api/v1/wages-jp/revisions?series=…: how one series changed from release to release.

Citing

Cite the publisher as the source, and Plover with the release you used and its frozen address. For example:

Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. Employment Referrals for General Workers:
active openings-to-applicants ratio, including part-time, seasonally adjusted,
January 1963 – September 2026. Plover Analytics, release “Data through September 2026”,
as of 30 October 2026.
https://ploveranalytics.com/api/v1/job-openings-jp/observations?series=sa.active.incl_part&as_of=2026-10-30&format=csv

Some publishers ask for a specific credit line, and the Bank of Japan's data comes with conditions of its own; each one is listed under Attribution in the Methodology.

Calculated Figures

Published values carry the Official Statistic label. Year-on-year, month-on-month and three-month annualised rates are calculated here from the published values, and state their formula under Show calculation and in every download. A rate calculated from rounded published indices can differ from the agency's own published rate by up to 0.1 percentage point; where the agency publishes the rate itself, use that figure. The Methodology gives every formula and the known breaks in each series.

Asking in Plain English

The question box on the front page answers from this site's data and names each figure's source and date. It helps to find the right series before loading it. A free account raises the number of questions you can ask in a day.
